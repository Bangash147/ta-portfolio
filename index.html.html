02 My Talent Acquisition Philosophy

A business partner, not a CV-processing queue

Eight principles that shape how I run every search. Each one replaces a habit that slows down technology hiring.

01 · Strategy

Hiring is a business function

Every search starts from the business outcome the role must deliver in 6–12 months, not from a copied job description.

02 · Quality

Quality of hire over volume

A short list of high-signal candidates beats 400 applicants. I measure the pipeline by fit and conversion, not count.

03 · Outbound

Proactive over passive

The best builders are rarely applying. I go where they publish work — repos, models, notebooks, communities.

04 · Experience

Candidates are future advocates

Clear stages, fast replies, real feedback. Every candidate — hired or not — should leave respecting the company.

05 · Partnership

Hiring managers as co-owners

Calibrated intake, agreed must-haves, shared scorecards and a weekly review. The search is a joint project.

06 · Fairness

Structured assessment

Same questions, anchored ratings, independent feedback before debrief. Fair to candidates and harder to fool.

07 · Evidence

Data-driven decisions

Funnel conversion, source quality and drop-off tell me where to fix the process — not opinions after the fact.

08 · Relationships

Long-term talent relationships

A “not now” is the start of a relationship. Warm pipelines mean the next search never starts from zero.

04 Technical Talent Acquisition

A real first technical conversation — without pretending to be an engineer

I am an HR and Talent Acquisition professional, not a software engineer. My job in a first conversation is to understand what a candidate actually built and owned, so that engineers spend their time only on candidates worth validating in depth.

The project deep-dive (20–30 min)

Five questions about one project the candidate is proud of:

1What did you build? The problem, the users, the scale.
2What was your part? “I” vs “we” — what they personally designed, wrote, decided.
3Why that stack? Can they explain trade-offs, not just list tools?
4What broke? A real failure, how they debugged it, what changed.
5What was the impact? Latency, cost, users, quality — and what they’d do differently.

What I validate

  • Ownership and scope of contribution
  • Can explain technical choices in plain language
  • Problem-solving narrative and learning speed
  • Evidence of shipping (repos, demos, models, products)
  • Motivation, role fit, compensation, notice period
  • Communication and collaboration signals

Signals I listen for

  • Specific numbers and constraints
  • Honest about what didn’t work
  • Curiosity about our problems

What engineers validate

  • Depth of algorithmic and system-design skill
  • Code quality in a work sample or pairing session
  • ML fundamentals, research rigour, evaluation design
  • Architecture judgement at our scale
  • Technical bar for the level being hired

How I prepare engineers

  • Screening summary with evidence and open questions
  • Agreed scorecard and focus area per interviewer
  • Feedback form due within 24 hours

Candidate evaluation scorecard — ML Engineer

Illustrative · synthetic candidate
CompetencyWeightAnchor for “4 — strong”RaterScore
ML depth30%Explains model and training choices with trade-offs and evidenceML lead
Engineering quality20%Work sample is readable, tested, reasonably efficientEngineer
Problem solving20%Breaks down an unfamiliar problem and checks assumptionsEngineer
Ownership15%Clear examples of driving work to done without being askedHM
Communication15%Explains technical work clearly to a non-specialistRecruiter

Scale 1–5 with written anchors for 1, 3 and 5. Weighted score informs the debrief; it never replaces it.

Why structure works

  • Every candidate is assessed against the same criteria
  • Anchored ratings reduce “gut feel” and halo effects
  • Independent scores before debrief prevent groupthink
  • Evidence notes make decisions explainable to candidates and founders
  • Data from scorecards improves the process over time

Non-negotiable

No AI system makes or recommends a hire, rejection or ranking. Tools may organise evidence; people decide.

Culture & collaboration

Assessed against stated values — ownership, hunger to learn, shipping and fixing, transparency — not “culture fit” or similarity to the panel.

Behavioural & competency questions

  • Ownership: Tell me about something you shipped that nobody asked you to build.
  • Learning: What did you learn in the last 3 months that changed how you work?
  • Failure: Describe a release that broke. What did you do in the first hour?
  • Trade-offs: When did you choose speed over quality, and was it right?
  • Collaboration: Tell me about a disagreement with a designer or PM and how it ended.
  • Transparency: When did you raise a problem early that others hadn’t seen?
  • Growth: Who have you helped get better at their job?

Probe with STAR: situation, task, action, result — and “what would you do differently?”

08 Responsible AI in hiring

Privacy, bias and limits — designed in, not bolted on

Privacy & data protection

  • Redact names, contacts, photos before any AI step
  • Use company-approved AI accounts with data-retention controls — never personal accounts
  • Candidate consent and clear privacy notice
  • Align with ImagineArt’s GDPR and SOC 2 commitments and local law
  • Delete on request; defined retention period

Bias mitigation

  • Prompts exclude protected characteristics and school prestige
  • Evidence against job-related criteria only
  • No scores, rankings or rejection suggestions from AI
  • Monitor pass-through rates by stage and source
  • Periodic audit of AI-drafted JDs and outreach for biased language

Limitations

  • Can misread or invent details — every output is verified
  • Rewards keywords; misses potential and non-traditional paths
  • Cannot judge technical depth — engineers do
  • Knowledge may be outdated on markets and pay
  • Not a substitute for talking to the candidate

Where AI helps most

  • Turning intake notes into a clear scorecard
  • Search strings and channel ideas in minutes
  • Consistent screening summaries for every candidate
  • Interview kits mapped to competencies
  • Consolidating panel notes and weekly reports

6 · Resign & counterofferPrepare the candidate before they resign
→
7 · Confirm joiningWeekly contact through notice; day-one plan

Objection handling

ObjectionResponse approach
“The salary is below my expectation”Revisit their stated priorities; explain the full package and growth path; escalate only with market data and equity in mind
“My company made a counteroffer”Return to why they started looking; most reasons for leaving remain after a raise; respect their decision
“A startup feels risky”Share public facts (bootstrapped, $35M+ ARR, global customers) and let them talk to the team
“My notice period is 3 months”Agree a realistic date; plan pre-joining engagement; consider buy-out only if policy allows
“I’m waiting on another offer”Ask the timeline and decision criteria; move fast without pressure tactics

Closing principles

  • Sell honestly: the mission, the work and the growth — and the hard parts
  • Know the candidate’s top 3 motivators before an offer exists
  • No exploding offers or pressure tactics
  • Compensation benchmarked for engineering and research roles
  • Joining is the finish line — not the signature

16 My Strategic Hiring Recommendation for ImagineArt

ImagineArt Builder Signal Network

A proof-of-work talent community that finds emerging AI engineers, independent builders and product creators by what they build — before they ever apply. Presented as an idea to validate against what ImagineArt already runs (for example, its talent pool and Student Ambassador programme).

The business problem

  • The strongest AI builders are rarely job-hunting — and are visible through their work, not CVs
  • CVs are weak signals for people without traditional pedigree, yet the company values hunger over pedigree
  • Competition for AI talent is global; a local brand among builders compounds over time
  • Every role filled from a warm, proven community is faster and lower-risk

Components

Monthly build challenges

Small, real problems using ImagineArt’s tools or APIs

Project showcases

Builders demo work to engineers monthly

University & lab links

Research talks, thesis problems, fellowships

Open-source discovery

Track contributors to relevant repos and models

Hackathon partners

Sponsor, judge, follow up same week

Opt-in community

Discord with explicit consent and clear rules

Portfolio submissions

“Show us what you built” instead of a CV

Work-based evaluation

Rubrics on shipped work; fast-track to interviews

16 Builder Signal Network — pilot plan

A 90-day pilot, measured like a product

Execution

  • Weeks 1–3: validate with founders; audit existing community activity
  • Weeks 4–6: first build challenge with 2 university labs + open call
  • Weeks 7–10: showcase night; fast-track top builders to interviews
  • Weeks 11–13: review results; decide to scale, change or stop

Resources

  • TA lead (owner) — about 20% of time
  • Two engineers as judges — ~4 hours a month each
  • Small prize and API-credit budget
  • Existing Discord or a community tool; ATS tags for tracking

Candidate experience

  • Short, fun, paid-in-credits challenges — never free labour on real roadmap work
  • Every submission gets feedback

Benefits

  • Warm pipeline of proven builders for AI/ML, engineering and design
  • Evidence-based hiring for non-traditional talent
  • Stronger employer brand among Pakistan’s builder community
  • Product feedback from power users who build

Success metrics

Risks & limitations
